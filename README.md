From behind the breakwater, the water is calm enough to read
the day twice. The Breakwater Post is the harbour edition of
Tuesday 25 November 2026 — moss green and slate, thirty-seven
stories from nine regions, each one checked against the chart
before it was allowed inside: dated today, or carrying the
date it truly belongs to on its face.

The harbour opens on the strait it watches. Iran says three
tankers were hit by mines at Hormuz — entered under CLAIMED,
because from behind the breakwater you log what the lane
reports and mark it as a report until the lane confirms it.
Next berth: Poland, where NATO is sending early-warning jets
after Russian missiles landed there, with Chancellor Merz's
promise of complete support entered alongside. Third berth,
the political entry of the day: Indonesia's Vice-President
has resigned amid political strain — a single-source report,
entered as REPORTED exactly as it arrived, with nothing added
that the source did not carry.

Inside the harbour, the rest of the day rides at anchor.
Seven killed in Gaza, entered by role: a Hamas security
chief, three policemen, three siblings, one a child.
Baghdad's order to seize the Kurdish oilfields. A new count
of Russian war dead past 700,000. Fifty Boeing jets on a
Xiamen order. Twelve coral species brought back on the Great
Barrier Reef. Madrid's 5–1 comeback. Brussels' sanctions.
Storms closing on Thanksgiving travel. Seven firms targeted
over chip diversion. And the PCE report, due this day, its
line left open — no figure had landed when the harbour
closed, and the Post does not paint water where the chart
says none has been sounded.

What the breakwater kept out is listed, not hidden: a Mercury
mission story dated 23 November, climate talks from earlier
in the month, a Liverpool result from an earlier round, and
launch hype for a game with no release today. Nvidia's rise
rides inside under its true colours — MONDAY CLOSE, dated to
Monday's sailing.

For the reader on the quay: the day's eight heaviest figures
run along the top, each tagged with its card. The harbour
chart is pinned by card number. The tides column reads the
day from high water to low. Region counts cover only stories
actually inside the harbour.

Set two ways at the harbour office: `compose_breakwater.py`
lays the page out under a slate band with three leads abreast,
and again under a paper masthead with a wide lead; the duel
is kept with the build notes, and the banded setting stands —
three berths, side by side, like the harbour itself.
`main.py` carries the finished page to `/output/entry.png`
byte for byte.

Wires: RFE/RL, Al Jazeera, DW, Starlit Daily, Forbes.
